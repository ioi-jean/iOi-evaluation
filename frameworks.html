Impact Strategy 2026

To demonstrate impact on people's lives, some form of data needs to be collected from participants — whether that's surveys, interviews, focus groups (face to face or online), personal diaries, images, a voice notes app, you name it. Evaluations rightly use many of the methods used in research projects.

Children as researchers

Children as researchers and interviewers of each other — with a process and guidance on using mics, recording equipment and drawing as research.

Things to consider

  • Measurement is usually taken after 1 day, or after weeks, of engagement — it doesn't really tell us what the programme itself achieved over time.
  • People want to know the details of the methodologies used, and why.
  • It's important to use tried-and-tested, sector-wide evaluation methods.
  • Aiming for more detailed case studies.
  • Short and relevant quotes from adults and children — covering resilience, skills, mental health, school.
  • Evaluation from staff and volunteers, not just participants.

Roadmap

Building storytelling and evaluation capability over three horizons.

Short-Term

  • Define what impact means for iOi.
  • Create a standard impact story collection form.
  • Adopt the STAR model for case studies.
  • Develop ethical storytelling guidelines.
  • Identify 5–10 strong impact stories already held within the organisation.
  • Begin collecting alumni stories.

Medium-Term

  • Develop audience-specific messaging.
  • Establish content pillars.
  • Create an impact story library.
  • Build an annual storytelling calendar.

Long-Term

  • Embed storytelling into evaluation and participation processes.
  • Create a 20-year legacy campaign using stories from past participants.
  • Develop a sustainable content waterfall process that turns every major project into multiple pieces of content and evidence.
