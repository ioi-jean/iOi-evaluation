One Imagination Cycle, three places

Every region measures children moving through the same five-stage cycle of voice and agency — the activity, age group and tools differ, the cycle doesn't.

Choose

“You choose, we listen and follow your lead”

Children make real decisions about what happens next in a session.

Ask

“We ask, because your ideas matter to us”

Children are listened to — their questions and interests shape what comes next.

Build

“We build it together, your ideas in our hands”

They create something themselves through hands-on, playful making.

Do

“We do it, turning your ideas into action”

They act on their choices and see an idea through to a result.

Show

“We show you the difference you've made”

They share what they made and learned, and are heard by others.

London

Tower Hamlets

Where Power of Play began. Built on existing hub-school relationships and a consortium of Young V&A, Easy Peasy, Save the Children and Ambition Aspire Achieve — now the programme's centre for learning and best-practice sharing.

A 500-child longitudinal study

500 children aged 8–11, and their parents and carers, are followed over the life of the programme to show what sustained access to creative play does over time — not just in a single block of sessions. Children and carers also co-design new ways to have their views on learning-through-play heard.

Baseline — before Year 1

Full toolkit

All 500 children, plus a parent/carer survey, before their first year of sessions.

Measures

DAT form, 5-question confidence survey, and facilitator observation with every child; an accompanying survey for parents and carers.

Each year of the programme

Repeat toolkit

The same 500 children, tracked annually.

Measures

DAT form, confidence survey and facilitator observation repeated at the end of each year's block, alongside co-designed qualitative voice methods with children and carers.

Things to consider

Because change is measured against the same cohort every year, attrition matters — track and report drop-off, and note where a child's circumstances (school move, family change) might explain a dip independent of the programme.

Programme end — Year 5

Longitudinal comparison

The headline evidence for the national policy case.

Measures

Full five-year comparison against baseline across all measures, plus the child/carer-designed voice tools trialled here and offered on to Doncaster and, in adapted form, West Belfast.

Northern Ireland

West Belfast

Delivered with Blackie River Community Groups (BRCG), a Sure Start provider working with 0–4 year olds and their parents in West Belfast.

Building local capacity from the ground up

Year one trains local early years practitioners in iOi's co-creation approach, delivers 3 Days of Play in BRCG's centres, and stands up a West Belfast co-design group; BRCG staff then take over delivery themselves. The Easy Peasy app extends playful home-learning to 1,000 families, and the approach extends to rural partners and into policy conversations as the co-design group is supported to advocate.

Practitioners

Train the trainer

Early years practitioners trained in iOi's co-creation and learning approach.

Measures

Numbers of practitioners trained, tracked in the shared spreadsheet tracker; iOi runs train-the-trainer sessions and shares iOi techniques and toolkits to support it.

Days of Play & co-design group

Partner-chosen tool

3 Days of Play in year one, plus a new West Belfast co-design group.

Measures

BRCG selects an outcome-tracking tool that fits early years — DAT, Net Promoter Score, or the LEGO Children Tool — alongside a child-voice tracker to understand levels of child voice gathered, and a log of co-design sessions run.

Things to consider

iOi is deliberately not prescribing a single tool here — the reporting guidance asks BRCG what they already ask parents/carers and children, and whether a digital observation tool already exists, so the method fits early years rather than being ported straight from Tower Hamlets.

Easy Peasy app

Reach tracking

Up to 1,000 families reached at home.

Measures

Easy Peasy engagement numbers, logged in the spreadsheet tracker alongside the other quantitative delivery and reach figures.

Rural extension & policy influence

Later in Year 1+

Extending reach to rural partners, and supporting the co-design group to advocate with policy-makers.

Measures

Same reporting approach applied as reach extends into rural communities; planning and co-creation for further years brings in parents, carers and policy-makers directly.

Reporting rhythm

Monthly check-in

How progress gets back to iOi for National Lottery reporting.

Measures

A shared spreadsheet tracker covers quantitative data (delivery, reach, engagement, outcomes) and qualitative data (co-design and child voice), reviewed with iOi at a monthly check-in alongside risks, challenges and successes.

South Yorkshire

Doncaster

Delivered with Harrison College, a specialist Further Education provider for students aged 16+ centred on inclusive education for students with SEN.

FE students become play mentors

Harrison College students deliver four Days of Play in local primary schools and are trained to take on delivery independently. The co-design approach is shared with FE students to help them engage more parents and carers, and the model is showcased at Doncaster festivals and events, including TEDx and the Remake Learning Festival.

FE mentors

Pre/post survey

Harrison College students across training and four Days of Play.

Measures

Pre/post confidence & skills survey, plus observation of their delivery once they're running sessions independently.

Things to consider

Because Harrison College prioritises inclusive, SEN-centred education, observation and voice tools here should be reviewed against BRCG's and Tower Hamlets' co-design learning so they work for students with additional needs too.
