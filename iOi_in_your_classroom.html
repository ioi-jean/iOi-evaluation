iOi evaluation guide

iOi in your Classroom

Evaluation approach: not yet decided — options below

Unlike a facilitated cohort programme, iOi in your Classroom is a digital platform: teachers self-select activities (Junk Bots, Natural Inks, Nature's Engineers and more) and run them in their own classroom, in their own time, with no iOi facilitator in the room and no fixed group or week structure. That changes what an evaluation toolkit can realistically ask for. This page sketches three ways to build one, plus how the Imagination Cycle could still travel with it.

Three ways to build the toolkit

Option A — Teacher self-report survey

Lowest burden

A short before/after survey the teacher completes about their class, attached to whichever activity they've downloaded.

What it measures

Teacher-perceived shift in pupil confidence, creativity and engagement — a class-level snapshot rather than individual child data.

Tools

A 5-question survey, triggered once when an activity is downloaded and again a set period later — e.g. one week on.

Trade-offs

Easiest to scale across every school and activity, and works whether a teacher runs the activity once or repeatedly. But it's a teacher's impression, not the child's voice, and can't be broken down by individual pupil.

Option B — Survey + platform usage data

Low burden

Option A's survey, triangulated with what the platform already knows: which activities are downloaded, how often, and by whom.

What it measures

Adds reach and dosage — how many schools/teachers are engaging, which activities land best, whether teachers come back for more — alongside the same perceived-impact survey.

Tools

The Option A survey, plus a light analytics pull (downloads by activity, region, repeat use) — no extra ask of the teacher beyond the survey itself.

Trade-offs

Good for reporting scale and reach to funders alongside perceived impact. Usage data shows engagement, not learning, so it strengthens rather than replaces the survey.

Option C — Imagination Cycle observations

Medium burden

The teacher observes their own class during the activity, using the same Choose/Ask/Build/Do/Show observations used on facilitated programmes.

What it measures

Direct(ish) evidence of the behaviours the Imagination Cycle names, seen by the person actually in the room — closer to child voice than a teacher's general impression.

Tools

A one-page observation/prompt card, built into each activity's teacher guide, with space for a few quick notes rather than a form to fill in properly.

Trade-offs

Keeps the same evaluation language and gives richer, more specific evidence. But it depends on a busy teacher's time and willingness to note things down mid-activity, and won't produce a clean comparable score across schools.

Hybrid — worth considering

Medium burden

Not a fourth separate option, but a combination flagged for discussion.

Shape

Option B's survey-plus-usage-data as the default for every teacher (low-friction, always-on), with the Option C observation card offered as an optional add-on for teachers who want to go deeper — or for a small sample iOi selects for closer case-study attention.

Why flag it

It gets baseline reach-and-perception data from everyone without asking too much, while still keeping a route to richer, cycle-aligned evidence where it's wanted — without committing every teacher to the heavier ask.

The Imagination Cycle, in a self-directed classroom

The cycle itself is reusable as-is — it was designed to travel across programmes. What changes for iOi in your Classroom is who's holding it: instead of an iOi facilitator filling in an observation form over four weeks, it's a teacher glancing at a prompt card during a single activity (this is what Option C, or the optional add-on in the hybrid, would draw on).

Choose

“You choose, we listen and follow your lead” “I believe my idea is worth trying”

Ask the child

  • What made you pick that over the other ideas?
  • If you could only do one thing today, what would it be?
  • Is there something you changed your mind about?

Look for

  • Makes a decision without being told what to do
  • Can give a reason for the choice, even a simple one
  • Changes their mind and can say why

Ask

“We ask, because your ideas matter to us” “I'm curious enough to watch and wonder”

Ask the child

  • What do you wonder about this?
  • What do you think will happen if you try that?
  • Is there anything you're still not sure about?

Look for

  • Asks questions without being prompted
  • Watches or studies something before jumping in
  • Makes a guess or prediction

Build

“We build it together, your ideas in our hands” “I'm ready to have a go”

Ask the child

  • How are you putting your idea together?
  • What's the tricky bit?
  • Who are you building this with?

Look for

  • Hands-on, trying things out
  • Keeps going after something doesn't work first time
  • Works alongside or with another child

Do

“We do it, turning your ideas into action” “I belong here, so I take the lead”

Ask the child

  • What did you decide to do next?
  • Did you lead this bit, or did someone else?
  • What would you do differently if you did it again?

Look for

  • Takes the initiative / leads a task
  • Carries an idea through to a finished result
  • Takes responsibility for how it turned out

Show

“We show you the difference you've made” “I tell others my views and help others use their imagination”

Ask the child

  • What do you want to tell people about what you made?
  • What would you tell a friend who wants to try this?
  • What are you proud of?

Look for

  • Explains or presents their work to someone else
  • Helps another child with theirs
  • Expresses pride or reflects on how they've changed

Touchpoints within a single activity

Because there's no fixed cohort or weekly cadence, the natural unit here is one activity rather than one week — whether a teacher runs it in a single lesson or spreads it across several. These touchpoints apply whichever option (or hybrid) is chosen above.

Before — download / sign-up

The moment a teacher picks up an activity.

Depending on the option chosen

  • Options A/B/Hybrid: baseline survey questions (5-question confidence style)
  • Option C: none needed — the observation card is used live, not as a baseline

Things to consider

Keep this to under a minute — a long form here risks teachers abandoning before they even start the activity.

During — running the activity

Whatever the teacher notices while pupils are hands-on.

Depending on the option chosen

  • Option C/Hybrid: the Imagination Cycle observation card, used loosely across the session rather than tied to a specific stage
  • Options A/B: nothing required mid-activity — this is where the low-burden design pays off

Things to consider

A teacher running the activity solo can't step back and observe the way a dedicated facilitator can — the card needs to work as something glanced at, not filled in continuously.

After — once the activity's finished

The follow-up point, however long after "before" is set.

Depending on the option chosen

  • Options A/B/Hybrid: repeat the short survey
  • Option C: a few closing notes on the observation card, or a short reflection prompt for pupils if the teacher has time

Things to consider

Decide how long after "before" this should sit — immediately (captures excitement, less considered) vs a week later (more considered, but lower response rate as teachers move on).

Over time — repeat use

For teachers who come back for a second or third activity.

Depending on the option chosen

  • Options B/Hybrid: usage data shows this automatically — repeat downloads, activities tried in sequence
  • Any option: comparing survey responses activity-to-activity gives a light-touch version of a before/after trend, without needing a fixed cohort

Things to consider

This is opt-in by nature of the platform, so the sample of teachers who do this repeatedly will be self-selecting (likely the most engaged), not representative.

This is a starting sketch, not a finished plan — the right option depends on what iOi can realistically ask of teachers, and what evidence matters most for reporting on iOi in your Classroom.
